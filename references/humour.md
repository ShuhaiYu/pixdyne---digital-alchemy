# humour.md — Blog writing reference

> Adapted from *"Writing Humor: The Art of Making Readers Laugh"* (Hire a Writer).
> We are writing this **because the old blog was too dry** — correct, tidy, and
> forgettable. Humour and personality are the fix. The article's five tips are
> permission, not restriction. What we change is the *register*: we want sharp,
> confident, human wit with real personality — closer to a good columnist than a
> corporate blog. Have fun with it. See [voice.md](./voice.md).

---

## 1. The mandate: loosen up

The previous posts read like a manual. The whole point of this reference set is to
give writers room to have a voice. So the default has flipped:

> Old default: "play it safe, stay neutral." → **New default: bring personality;
> let the prose have a pulse.**

Humour here does not mean jokes-per-paragraph. It means the post sounds like a
real, sharp person wrote it — someone with opinions, a dry sense of timing, and the
confidence to say the obvious thing plainly. That confidence reads as wit even when
no joke is told.

---

## 2. Where the energy comes from

One thing does **not** change: the energy has to be *earned*, not faked. The
audience was burned by vendors who oversold (CLAUDE.md §4), so the line we hold is
honesty, not restraint. So:

- **Lift the prose with:** voice, rhythm, a well-timed short sentence, a vivid
  concrete image, an unexpected-but-true observation, a confident opinion
  ([opinions.md](./opinions.md)), a small story ([stories.md](./stories.md)), and a
  punchy exclamation when a moment truly calls for it.
- **Don't fake it with:** "best/leading/world-class", hype adjectives, or invented
  numbers. Those are dishonest, not just loud.

"Lively" and "honest" are not in tension — the liveliest writing on earth is also
the most precise. (Exclamation marks are fine now; AGENTS.md §3.4 was updated
2026-06-24. Just don't lean on them.)

---

## 3. The five tips, as permission

**1. Be yourself.** Our self is the plain-spoken Melbourne operator who has seen it
all and is slightly amused by the same mistakes happening again. Write *as that
person*, with their dry humour intact — not as a brand committee.

**2. Keep it light-hearted; avoid charged topics.** Still true. Wit, not edge. No
politics, religion, nationality, gender, or punching down. Be playful about *the
work and its absurdities*, never about people.

**3. Use universal references.** Skip memes and celebrities — they age and exclude.
Our universal material is **the shared comedy of the work itself**: the plugin
graveyard, the spreadsheet nobody trusts, the "quick" project the last vendor left
half-finished. Every reader in the audience nods at those.

**4. Keep it audience-appropriate.** PG and professional. The test: would it still
read well if the reader's CFO saw it? Sharp is good; crude or flippant about their
money is not.

**5. Sprinkle, don't slather.** The article's most durable point — and it cuts
*both* ways now. The old blog under-seasoned to the point of blandness; the fix is
not to drown every line in quips. A few well-placed dry moments per post make the
whole thing feel alive. Aim for *seasoned*, not *bland* and not *clowning*.

---

## 4. What good Pixdyne wit sounds like

Dry, confident, true. The humour rides on a hard truth stated plainly, often with a
twist on a cliché:

> "Migrating messy data instead of cleaning it first — garbage in, garbage forever."

> "An agent that confidently invents an answer is worse than no agent at all."

> "Shopify is easy to start and easy to outgrow badly."

> "A Salesforce nobody updates is worse than the spreadsheet it replaced."

These are the *right* register — and the old blog had too few of them. Write more
lines like this: an honest observation with a little spin on it. You can also go a
notch warmer and more playful than these as long as it stays dry and on-brand.

---

## 5. The only hard lines

The guardrails are about truth and respect, not about suppressing personality:

- **No jokes at the expense of a named client, competitor, or vendor.** Be wry
  about *the problem*; never mock a *name* (§6 rules 1–2).
- **No puns on the brand names.** Pixdyne and OnlyPixAI spellings are sacred (§13);
  do not turn them into wordplay.
- **No "digital alchemy" gags.** It is a wordmark, not a punchline (AGENTS.md §3.3).
- **Don't let a joke smuggle in a falsehood.** Wit is never an excuse for an
  invented metric or claim ([stats.md](./stats.md); §6 rule 3).
- **AI positioning stays intact** even when being playful (§6 rule 7).

Inside those lines, you have a lot of room. Use it.

---

## 6. The keep-it test

A line earns its place if:

1. It sounds like a sharp, funny human — not a brand committee.
2. It rides on something *true*.
3. It cannot offend or exclude any reader (no punching down, no named targets).
4. It earns its energy honestly — wit and voice, not hype or invented claims.
5. It is one of a handful of moments, not wall-to-wall — seasoned, not bland, not clowning.

The bar is no longer "prove you deserve to be funny." It is "be human and witty —
just stay true, kind, and precise while you do it."

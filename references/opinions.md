# opinions.md — Blog writing reference

> A working reference for taking a stance in Pixdyne blog posts. The humour
> article's first tip — **be yourself** — is, underneath, advice about conviction:
> authentic writing has a point of view. CLAUDE.md §14.11 makes this a hard rule:
> *"Every post must have a clear, opinionated angle. No generic listicles."*
> See [voice.md](./voice.md) for tone.

---

## 1. Why an angle is mandatory

A post with no opinion is a brochure. It ranks badly, gets quoted by no AI engine,
and persuades no one. The whole site is a credibility surface — and credibility
comes from being willing to say *"here is what we actually think, and why,"* not
from hedged neutrality.

Our audience has been **burned by vendors who oversold** (CLAUDE.md §4). The most
trust-building thing we can do is give honest advice that sometimes argues *against*
our own short-term interest. That honesty **is** the angle.

---

## 2. The Pixdyne house angle: the honest default

The strongest move available to us — **state the honest default, then name the
exception.** It is contrarian without being contrarian for its own sake, and it
always lands on what we genuinely believe. The earlier posts often *had* this angle
but buried it under flat, manual-style prose. The fix is to surface it and lead with
it, with conviction.

> "For most businesses, an off-the-shelf CRM like Salesforce or HubSpot is the
> right starting point. A custom build only pays off in specific cases."

> "Before iOS versus Android, the real question is whether you need a native app
> at all."

> "A NetSuite implementation is not a software install — it is moving how your
> business runs onto one system."

> "We are not selling a build for its own sake — a good share of our work is
> configuring Salesforce or HubSpot properly so a custom CRM is never needed."

The pattern: **lead with the unglamorous, honest recommendation** (often "use the
cheaper, proven thing first"), which signals we are not just maximising the
invoice — *then* draw the precise line where the bigger build is genuinely right.
That second half is where we sell, and it lands because the first half earned it.

---

## 3. How to find the angle for a new post

Ask one of these:

- **What does everyone get wrong about this?** (e.g. "the platform is not the
  problem; how it is built and maintained is.")
- **What is the honest default most vendors won't admit?** (e.g. "start
  off-the-shelf, usually.")
- **What is the real first question, before the obvious one?** (e.g. "do you need
  an app at all?")
- **Where does this commonly fail, and why is it never the software's fault?**
  (every failure-mode section on the blog.)

If you cannot answer at least one, the topic is not ready to write.

## 4. Rules for opinions (the guardrails)

An opinion is a stance, not licence to break governance.

- **Defensible, not provocative.** Take a position you can back from the work, not
  a hot take for clicks. "Keep it light-hearted; avoid charged topics" from the
  humour article applies to opinions too.
- **Never trash a named competitor, vendor, or platform.** Be opinionated about
  *trade-offs* ("Webflow stops fitting once you need heavy e-commerce"), never
  derogatory about a *name*. We critique fit, not companies (§6 rules 1–2).
- **No superlatives as opinions.** "Best", "leading", "the only way" are banned
  (§14.7). A real opinion is specific and conditional, not a boast.
- **Argue from what we actually do.** Every stance must trace back to how Pixdyne
  genuinely works since 2018. Do not invent a philosophy we don't practise.
- **No size segmentation, no engineer-only jargon** in the published copy (§6
  rules 9–10) — even when stating a strong view.
- **AI positioning is fixed, not an opinion to relitigate** (§6 rule 7): AI is a
  capability we deliver to clients. Do not voice a "take" that contradicts it.
- **Back non-obvious claims with a source.** A confident claim that is also
  *checkable* is far stronger — see [stats.md](./stats.md).

---

## 5. Opinion ≠ certainty about facts

Be opinionated about **judgement** ("usually start off-the-shelf"); be careful
about **facts** ("Google introduced BERT in 2019"). The first is a defensible
stance; the second must be true and ideally cited. Never dress a fabricated metric
as a bold opinion — that is a §6 rule 3 violation wearing a disguise.

## 6. Quick check

- [ ] Can I state this post's angle in one sentence?
- [ ] Does the angle lead with an honest default before the upsell?
- [ ] Is it defensible from how we actually work, not a hot take?
- [ ] Does it critique trade-offs, never a named company?
- [ ] No superlatives, no banned framing, AI positioning intact?
- [ ] Are the factual claims under the opinion true and, where non-obvious, cited?
